# The ENTIRE Israel, Palestine and Iran Conflict, Explained


## HOOK

**001 · FEB 28, 2026** (3D) — On the 28th of February 2026, American and Israeli strikes killed the man who had ruled Iran for 36 years.
**002 · HORMUZ** (3D) — Iran fired missiles at Israel and at Gulf states hosting American bases. Then it began closing the Strait of Hormuz, the route for about a fifth of the world's oil.
**003 · HOW?** (MAP) — So how did a fight over land only a little larger than New Jersey drag in Iran, America and the world's oil? And why does every ceasefire seem to break?
**004 · ONE BUILDING** (CHAR) — To answer that, we have to start with one building in Tehran. In February 1979, the flag on it changed.
**005 · 1948 → 2026** (CHAR) — Why it changed is part of a story that starts thirty years earlier, with two peoples claiming the same land. This is that whole story, told from every side.

## I. ONE LAND, TWO PEOPLES

**101 · CHAPTER I** (MAP) — Chapter one. One land, two peoples.
**101a · WHY THIS LAND?** (MAP) — For many Jews, this was an ancestral homeland and a hoped-for refuge from persecution. Palestinian Arabs also had deep ties to the land, where their families had lived for generations, and wanted a nation of their own. Both peoples claimed a national future in the same place.
**102 · 1947** (MAP) — After the Second World War, Britain was ready to leave the land it had ruled as Mandatory Palestine. Jews and Arabs both lived there, and both wanted it as their home.
**103 · 33 · 13 · 10** (MAP) — In November 1947, the United Nations voted on a plan to split it into two states, a Jewish one and an Arab one, with Jerusalem under international control. Thirty-three countries voted yes, thirteen voted no, and ten abstained.
**104 · YES / NO** (CHAR) — Jewish leaders accepted the plan as a path to a state. Arab leaders rejected it, arguing that the Arab majority should decide the future of the whole land.
**105 · MAY 1948** (MAP) — Fighting broke out. In May 1948, Israel declared independence, and the next day the armies of neighbouring Arab states entered the war.
**106 · TWO NAMES** (CHAR) — Israelis remember this as their War of Independence. Palestinians call it the Nakba, the catastrophe. Around 700,000 to 750,000 Palestinians fled or were expelled from their homes.
**107 · 1949** (MAP) — When the fighting stopped in 1949, Israel held about 77 percent of the land. Egypt ran the Gaza Strip, and Jordan held the West Bank, including East Jerusalem. The Arab state from the UN plan never appeared.

## II. OCCUPATION AND OSLO

**201 · CHAPTER II** (MAP) — Chapter two. Occupation, uprising, and a handshake.
**201a · MAY 1967** (MAP) — By 1967 the region was tense again. Egypt moved troops into the Sinai and closed the Straits of Tiran to Israeli shipping. Israeli leaders feared a wider war.
**202 · 6 DAYS** (3D) — In June 1967, after weeks of rising tension with its neighbours, Israel struck first. In just six days it took Gaza and the Sinai from Egypt, the West Bank and East Jerusalem from Jordan, and the Golan Heights from Syria.
**203 · SINAI RETURNED** (MAP) — Israel later gave the Sinai back to Egypt in exchange for peace. But Gaza and the West Bank, home to millions of Palestinians, stayed under Israeli military control.
**204 · SETTLEMENTS** (MAP) — Over the following decades, Israel built settlements there. Today roughly 740,000 to 780,000 Israeli settlers live in the West Bank, including East Jerusalem. The UN's top court says these settlements break international law. Israel rejects that and says the land's future must be negotiated.
**205 · INTIFADA** (CHAR) — In 1987, Palestinians rose up against Israeli rule in what became known as the First Intifada, meaning shaking off. Strikes, protests and stone-throwing went on for years.
**205a · DAILY LIFE** (CHAR) — Over the following decades, for many Palestinians, life under occupation meant checkpoints, permits and soldiers at the end of the street. For many Israelis, it meant fear of attacks on buses and cafés. Civilians in both societies faced fear, loss and insecurity.
**206 · 1993** (CHAR) — Then came a surprise. In 1993, Israel and the Palestine Liberation Organization exchanged letters. The PLO recognised Israel. Israel recognised the PLO as the representative of the Palestinian people.
**207 · OSLO** (CHAR) — These were the Oslo Accords. They created the Palestinian Authority to govern parts of Gaza and the West Bank for five years, while the hardest questions were left for later: borders, Jerusalem, refugees and settlements.
**208 · LATER?** (CHAR) — Later never really came.
**208a · NOV 1995** (CHAR) — In 1995, Israel's prime minister Yitzhak Rabin, who had signed Oslo, was shot dead at a peace rally by an Israeli extremist who opposed the deal. His murder deepened the crisis facing a peace process already shaken by opposition and violence.
**208b · CAMP DAVID** (CHAR) — In the summer of 2000, American president Bill Clinton brought Israeli and Palestinian leaders to Camp David to settle everything at once. After two weeks, they left without a final agreement, and each side blamed the other for the failure.

## III. THE FLAG IN TEHRAN

**301 · CHAPTER III** (MAP) — Chapter three. The flag in Tehran.
**302 · QUIET PARTNERS** (CHAR) — Now back to that building. Before 1979, Iran was ruled by a king, the Shah. Iran and Israel were quiet partners, sharing intelligence, trade and military cooperation, even without a formal alliance.
**303 · REVOLUTION** (3D) — Then, in 1979, a revolution swept the Shah away, and Iran became an Islamic Republic. Its new leaders made opposition to Israel part of the state's mission.
**303a · 444 DAYS** (CHAR) — That same year, Iranian students stormed the American embassy in Tehran and held 52 Americans hostage for 444 days. Iran's revolutionary leaders turned openly hostile to both America and Israel.
**304 · SAME BUILDING** (CHAR) — Iran cut ties with Israel. And the Israeli embassy in Tehran was handed over to the PLO. The same building, a different flag. Iran's new leaders made the Palestinian cause part of their foreign policy.
**305 · 1982** (MAP) — In 1982, Israel invaded Lebanon to drive out PLO fighters. Iran's Revolutionary Guards went to Lebanon too, and helped found a new Shia group there: Hezbollah. Iran trained it, armed it and paid for it.
**306 · NOT OVERNIGHT** (CHAR) — Even so, the break was not overnight. During Iran's long war with Iraq in the 1980s, Israel still quietly sold Western weapons to Iran. Enemies in public, sometimes partners in secret.

## IV. WALLS AND CENTRIFUGES

**401 · CHAPTER IV** (MAP) — Chapter four. Walls, rockets and centrifuges.
**402 · 2000** (CHAR) — In 2000, peace talks collapsed and a Second Intifada began. This time it was far deadlier, Palestinian bombings and shootings killed Israelis, including civilians. Israeli military operations killed Palestinians, including civilians. It lasted about five years.
**402a · THE BARRIER** (MAP) — During those years, Israel began building a barrier of fences and concrete walls along and inside the West Bank. Israel says it stopped bombers from getting through. Palestinians say it cut them off from their land, and in 2004 the UN's top court called the parts inside the West Bank illegal.
**403 · 2005** (MAP) — In 2005, Israel pulled all 21 of its settlements and its permanent troops out of Gaza. About 8,500 settlers left.
**404 · HAMAS** (CHAR) — A year later, Hamas, an Islamist movement that refuses to recognise Israel, won Palestinian parliamentary elections. In 2007, after fighting its rival Fatah, it took full control of Gaza. Fatah's Palestinian Authority kept governing parts of the West Bank.
**405 · BLOCKADE** (MAP) — Israel tightened a blockade around Gaza, and Egypt restricted its own border crossing. Israel said it was stopping weapons from reaching Hamas. Palestinians called it collective punishment of two million people.
**406 · 2008 · 2014 · 2021** (MAP) — Rockets from Gaza and Israeli strikes became a cycle. There were major wars in 2008, 2014 and 2021, each ending in a ceasefire that solved nothing underneath.
**406a · 15 SECONDS** (CHAR) — In Israeli towns near Gaza, families had about fifteen seconds to reach a shelter when the sirens went off. In Gaza, two million people lived behind the blockade, most unable to leave, with power cuts for much of the day.
**407 · NATANZ** (3D) — Meanwhile, the fight with Iran moved underground. Iran was enriching uranium at a site called Natanz. In 2010, a computer worm named Stuxnet was found inside, built to sabotage the machines that spin the uranium.
**408 · THE DEAL** (CHAR) — In 2015, Iran and world powers signed a deal. Iran limited its enrichment and accepted inspections, in exchange for relief from sanctions. In 2018, President Trump pulled America out and brought the sanctions back.
**409 · SHADOW WAR** (CHAR) — Then came the shadow war: sabotage, cyberattacks and assassinations. In 2020, Iran's top nuclear scientist was killed near Tehran. Iran blamed Israel. Israel did not claim it.

## V. OCTOBER 7 AND GAZA

**501 · CHAPTER V** (MAP) — Chapter five. October the 7th, and the war in Gaza.
**502 · OCT 7, 2023** (MAP) — On the 7th of October 2023, Hamas-led fighters broke through the fence around Gaza and attacked towns, army posts and a music festival in southern Israel.
**503 · 1,200 · 251** (CHAR) — According to Israel, about 1,200 people were killed, most of them civilians, and 251 people were taken back into Gaza as hostages. It was the deadliest day in Israel's history.
**504 · WAR** (MAP) — Israel declared war, with two goals: destroy Hamas and bring the hostages home. What followed was a massive air and ground campaign in one of the most crowded places on Earth.
**504a · NOV 2023** (CHAR) — In late November 2023, a one-week truce freed more than a hundred hostages in exchange for Palestinian prisoners. Then the fighting resumed, and moved south.
**505 · 74,746** (MAP) — By late November 2026, Gaza's Health Ministry, part of the Hamas-run government, reported at least 74,746 Palestinians killed. Its count does not separate civilians from fighters, and Israel disputes it.
**506 · 90% DISPLACED** (MAP) — Nearly 1.9 million people, about nine in ten Gazans, were driven from their homes, many of them again and again.
**507 · FAMINE?** (CHAR) — In August 2025, the UN-backed food experts of the IPC confirmed famine in Gaza City and the area around it. Israel rejected the finding and challenged the data.
**508 · THE COURT** (CHAR) — South Africa took Israel to the International Court of Justice, accusing it of genocide. Israel denies it and says it is defending itself against Hamas. The court ordered Israel to prevent genocidal acts, but it has not ruled on the accusation itself. That decision is still years away.
**508a · OCT 2024** (MAP) — In October 2024, Israeli troops killed Yahya Sinwar, the Hamas leader in Gaza who had planned October 7th. The war did not stop.

## VI. THE RING OF FIRE

**601 · CHAPTER VI** (MAP) — Chapter six. The ring of fire.
**602 · THE AXIS** (MAP) — Around Israel stood groups backed by Iran: Hezbollah in Lebanon, militias in Iraq and Syria, the Houthis in Yemen, and Hamas itself. Iran calls them the axis of resistance. Each has its own leaders and its own goals, but Iran helped arm them all.
**603 · THE NORTH** (MAP) — The day after October 7th, Hezbollah began firing at northern Israel in support of Gaza. Israel fired back. Tens of thousands of people on both sides of the border had to leave their homes.
**604 · THE PAGERS** (3D) — Then, in September 2024, thousands of pagers used by Hezbollah members exploded almost at the same moment. The next day, it was walkie-talkies. Israel's prime minister later confirmed he had approved the operation.
**605 · NASRALLAH** (MAP) — Ten days later, an Israeli airstrike in Beirut killed Hassan Nasrallah, Hezbollah's leader for 32 years.
**605a · INTO LEBANON** (MAP) — Israeli troops then crossed into southern Lebanon. In late November 2024, a ceasefire ended the main fighting, though Israeli strikes in Lebanon continued, with Israel saying it was targeting Hezbollah.
**606 · RED SEA** (3D) — Far to the south, the Houthis in Yemen started attacking ships in the Red Sea, saying they were acting for the Palestinians. Shipping companies sent their vessels the long way around Africa. Traffic through the strait off Yemen fell by more than half.
**607 · ASSAD FALLS** (MAP) — And in December 2024, rebels toppled Bashar al-Assad in Syria. Iran lost its land bridge to Hezbollah. In just a few months, Iran's ring had been badly broken.

## VII. FACE TO FACE

**701 · CHAPTER VII** (MAP) — Chapter seven. Face to face.
**702 · APRIL 2024** (MAP) — For forty-five years, Iran and Israel had fought through others. That ended in April 2024. After a strike on Iran's embassy compound in Damascus, widely blamed on Israel, Iran launched more than 300 drones and missiles straight at Israel.
**703 · INTERCEPTED** (MAP) — Most were shot down by Israel and its allies, and Israel answered with a limited strike.
**703a · TEHRAN, JULY 2024** (MAP) — Then, in July, Hamas's political leader, Ismail Haniyeh, was killed in a guesthouse in Tehran, where he had come for the new Iranian president's inauguration. Iran blamed Israel and promised retaliation. Months later, Israel's defence minister acknowledged the killing.
**703b · OCT 2024** (MAP) — In October, Iran fired about 180 ballistic missiles at Israel. Weeks later, Israel struck military targets inside Iran.
**704 · JUNE 13, 2025** (3D) — Then, on the 13th of June 2025, Israel launched a much bigger attack on Iran's nuclear sites, missile bases and military commanders. Israel said Iran was getting dangerously close to a nuclear weapon.
**705 · 60%** (CHAR) — Iran said its program was peaceful. It had enriched uranium to sixty percent, far beyond what power plants use, but having that material is not the same as having a bomb.
**706 · FORDOW** (3D) — Iran fired waves of missiles at Israeli cities. On the 22nd of June, America joined in, bombing three nuclear sites, including Fordow, buried deep inside a mountain.
**707 · 12 DAYS** (MAP) — After twelve days, a ceasefire announced by Washington took hold. Iran reported about 1,062 people killed. Israel reported 29. Nobody could say for sure how much of Iran's nuclear program survived underground.

## VIII. BROKEN DEALS

**801 · CHAPTER VIII** (MAP) — Chapter eight. Deals that don't hold.
**802 · JAN 2025** (CHAR) — In Gaza, a ceasefire in January 2025 swapped 33 Israeli hostages, living and dead, for nearly 1,800 Palestinian prisoners. In March, Israel resumed its strikes. Israel blamed Hamas for refusing more releases. Hamas said Israel had abandoned the deal's second phase.
**803 · OCT 2025** (CHAR) — In October 2025, a new ceasefire under President Trump's twenty-point plan brought the last living hostages home and let more aid in. Israeli troops pulled back part of the way.
**804 · STALLED** (MAP) — But the harder steps stalled: disarming Hamas, a full Israeli withdrawal, an international force, and rebuilding. Strikes and deaths continued, each side blaming the other.
**805 · 2026** (MAP) — And then, Iran. After the February strikes, a ceasefire came in April. America blockaded Iran's ports. In June, both sides signed a sixty-day deal and the blockade was lifted.
**806 · BLOCKADE BACK** (3D) — Within weeks, attacks on ships and new American strikes broke it. The blockade came back in July. As of late November 2026, talks continue in Qatar, and Iran is still closing Hormuz one day at a time.

## IX. WHY IT DOESN'T END

**901 · CHAPTER IX** (MAP) — So why doesn't it end?
**902 · ISRAEL'S GOVERNMENT** (CHAR) — Israel's government says it cannot be safe while Hamas is armed on its border, or while Iran can build missiles and get close to a bomb. It opposes a Palestinian state now, saying that would reward October 7th.
**903 · PALESTINIAN LEADERS** (CHAR) — The Palestinian Authority wants a state on the 1967 lines, with East Jerusalem as its capital, living in peace beside Israel. Its president has rejected October 7th and says Hamas must give up its weapons. Hamas demands a permanent end to the war and a full Israeli withdrawal.
**904 · IRAN'S GOVERNMENT** (CHAR) — Iran's government says it will never build a nuclear weapon, but it wants to keep enriching uranium, and it demands an end to the blockade, the sanctions and the attacks. Meanwhile, ordinary Iranian families live with the strikes and an economy squeezed by sanctions.
**905 · THE ANSWER** (CHAR) — So how did a strip of land pull in Iran, America and the world's oil? Because since 1979, the fight over that land and the conflict between Iran and Israel have been linked. And why do the ceasefires break? Because questions of security, land, sovereignty and power are still unresolved, and each government sees the other's moves as a threat.
**906 · ONE BUILDING** (CHAR) — In 1979, one flag came down in Tehran and another went up. From that day, the two conflicts became linked, and they have not come apart since.
