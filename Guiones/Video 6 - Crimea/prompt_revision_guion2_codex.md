04 [THE PARADOX] The answer is a paradox. Crimea gives Russia huge military value, but keeping it supplied depends on a few vulnerable links.
05 [THE PENINSULA] Crimea is a peninsula between the Black Sea and the Sea of Azov. Narrow strips of land, like the Perekop Isthmus, link it to mainland Ukraine.
*05a [EASY TO DEFEND] That makes Crimea easy to defend from the north, and hard to supply if those land links are cut.
06 [SEVASTOPOL] In the southwest sits Sevastopol: a deep, sheltered harbour that gives a navy a strategic base in the northern Black Sea.
*06a [WARM WATER] Many of Russia's ports freeze for part of the year. Sevastopol does not, and it is about a day's sailing from the Turkish Straits, the Black Sea's only exit to the Mediterranean.
*06b [SEA CONTROL] Whoever holds Crimea can watch shipping across the Black Sea and send warships toward the Mediterranean.
07 [CRIMEAN KHANATE] Crimea was not always Russian. For centuries it was home to the Crimean Tatars and their Khanate.
08 [1783] In seventeen eighty-three, the Russian Empire under Catherine the Great annexed it, and built Sevastopol as its new naval base.
09 [SAME PENINSULA] Seventy years later, Britain, France and the Ottoman Empire landed here in the Crimean War. They besieged Sevastopol for almost a year, and Russia lost the war.
*09a [1941–1942] In the Second World War, Sevastopol was besieged again. German and Romanian forces needed about eight months to take it, and the Red Army retook Crimea in nineteen forty-four.
10 [1944] In nineteen forty-four, Stalin ordered the deportation of Crimea's Tatar population, mainly to Central Asia.
*10a [EXILE] Around one hundred ninety thousand people were deported in a few days. Tens of thousands died in the first years of exile, and most could only return in the late nineteen-eighties.
11 [1954] Then, in nineteen fifty-four, Soviet leaders transferred Crimea from the Russian republic to the Ukrainian republic. Inside the Soviet Union, it was just an internal line.
12 [1991] But in nineteen ninety-one, the Soviet Union collapsed. That internal line became an international border, and Crimea stayed in independent Ukraine.
*12a [AUTONOMY] Crimea became an autonomous republic inside Ukraine, with a Russian-speaking majority, and a long argument over who owned the Black Sea Fleet.
*12b [1997 · 2010] In nineteen ninety-seven, the two countries divided the fleet. In twenty ten, Ukraine extended Russia's lease in Sevastopol until twenty forty-two, in exchange for cheaper gas.
*13 [THE LEASE] So Russia's main southern naval base sat inside another country, on a lease.
*13a [2014: KYIV] That only worked while Kyiv stayed friendly. In early twenty fourteen, mass protests in Kyiv toppled Ukraine's pro-Russian president.
*14 [2014] Days later, soldiers without insignia seized Crimea's parliament and key sites. Within weeks, Russia annexed the peninsula after a referendum most of the world rejected.
*14a [UN VOTE] At the United Nations, one hundred countries voted to affirm Ukraine's borders. Russia's president later admitted the soldiers were Russian troops.
15 [WATER CUT] Ukraine answered by blocking the North Crimean Canal. Before then, it had supplied most of Crimea's water, mainly for farming.
*15a [DRY FIELDS] Irrigated farming in northern Crimea collapsed. Rice fields dried out, and reservoirs ran low.
16 [NO DIRECT ROAD] Now Russia had a problem. It held Crimea, but had no direct road from Russia. Supplies relied heavily on ferries and shipping.
*16a [BLACKOUT] Crimea also relied on Ukraine for electricity. In November twenty fifteen, saboteurs blew up power lines in southern Ukraine, and much of Crimea lost power for weeks. Russia answered with undersea cables across the Kerch Strait.

*17a [EUROPE'S LONGEST] It became Europe's longest bridge, cost more than three billion dollars, and Russia's president opened it by driving a truck across.
18 [ROAD + RAIL] It is really two parallel bridges: one for cars and trucks, and one for trains carrying fuel, equipment and troops.
*18a [THE AZOV SQUEEZE] The bridge also changed the Sea of Azov. Ships heading to Ukrainian ports like Mariupol had to pass under its arch, and Russia began holding them for inspections.
*18b [NOV 2018] In November twenty eighteen, Russia blocked the strait with a tanker under the bridge, then fired on three Ukrainian navy boats and seized them with their crews.
19 [2022: INVASION] In February twenty twenty-two, Russia launched its full-scale invasion. Crimea became a launch pad for the attack on southern Ukraine.
20 [LAND CORRIDOR] Russian forces seized a land corridor along the Sea of Azov, giving Russia a road to Crimea, and reopened the canal.
