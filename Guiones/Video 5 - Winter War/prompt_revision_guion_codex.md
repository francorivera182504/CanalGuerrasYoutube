08 [FINLAND SAYS NO] He even offered a bigger area of forest in exchange. Finland refused to give up its main defensive line.
09 [MAINILA SHOTS] On November twenty-sixth, Moscow claimed Finnish artillery had shelled a Soviet village. Finland denied it, and historians widely see it as staged.
10 [NOVEMBER 30] Four days later, on November thirtieth, 1939, the Red Army crossed the border and bombers hit Helsinki.
11 [EXPELLED] Two weeks later, the invasion got the Soviet Union expelled from the League of Nations.
12 [A QUICK WAR?] Soviet planners expected a short campaign. They had far more men, tanks and planes.
13 [PUPPET GOVERNMENT] Moscow even created a puppet Finnish government, ready to move into Helsinki once the capital fell.
14 [PURGED OFFICERS] But just a few years earlier, Stalin's purges had removed thousands of experienced Red Army officers.
15 [THE MANNERHEIM LINE] Their main attack hit the Karelian Isthmus, where Finland had built the Mannerheim Line of bunkers, trenches and rocks.
16 [KOLLAA HOLDS] North of Lake Ladoga, small Finnish units held their positions so stubbornly that 'Kollaa holds' became a national slogan.
17 [CUT FINLAND IN HALF] Further north, Soviet divisions pushed through the forests, trying to cut Finland in half at its narrow waist.
18 [MINUS 40] But that winter was brutal, with temperatures falling to around minus forty degrees.
19 [ONLY ROADS] The Soviet columns were tied to a few narrow roads, stretched for kilometres through thick forest.
20 [SKI TROOPS] The Finns moved on skis, in white camouflage, attacking from the trees and disappearing again.
21 [MOTTI] They cut the columns into isolated pockets and called them motti, the Finnish word for a pile of logs waiting to be chopped.
22 [SUOMUSSALMI] At Suomussalmi in December, the Finns defeated one Soviet division. Then another division was sent to help.
23 [RAATE ROAD] In early January 1940, on the Raate Road, that division was trapped, split apart and shattered.
24 [LEFT BEHIND] The Finns captured tanks, guns and trucks they badly needed. Thousands of Soviet soldiers died in the cold and the fighting.
25 [THE WHITE DEATH] One Finnish sniper, Simo Häyhä, became a legend, credited with hundreds of kills in about three months.
26 [MOLOTOV COCKTAIL] Molotov claimed Soviet planes were dropping bread to the Finns. The Finns called the bombs Molotov's bread baskets, and answered with bottles of fuel: Molotov cocktails.
27 [THE WORLD WATCHED] Around the world, newspapers cheered the small country that kept stopping a giant.
28 [STALIN ADAPTS] But Stalin did not give up. He changed commanders, brought in more artillery and prepared a massive new attack.
29 [FEBRUARY 1940] In February, the Red Army hammered the Mannerheim Line with huge barrages, day after day.
30 [THE LINE BREAKS] The line finally broke near Summa, and the Finns fell back toward the city of Viipuri.
31 [RUNNING OUT] Finland was running short of men and ammunition, and promised help from Britain and France was too slow.
32 [MARCH 13, 1940] So Finland signed the Moscow Peace Treaty, and the fighting ended on March thirteenth, 1940, after one hundred and five days.
33 [A TENTH LOST] Finland lost around a tenth of its territory, including Viipuri, its second largest city.
34 [EVACUATED] More than four hundred thousand Finns left their homes and moved west rather than live under Soviet rule.
35 [THE PRICE] Around twenty-six thousand Finnish soldiers died. Well over a hundred thousand Soviet troops were killed or went missing.
36 [STILL INDEPENDENT] But Finland was never occupied. It kept its government, its army and its independence.
37 [HITLER WATCHED] And the war had another effect. The Red Army looked weak, and Hitler took note.
38 [LESSONS LEARNED] The Soviets learned from their mistakes and began rebuilding their army.
39 [1941] A year later, Germany invaded the Soviet Union. Finland joined the fight to win back its lost land.
